The 30-minute Sunday breakfast routine

A short Sunday session that covers breakfast for the whole week. Three formats, real timings, and the honest list of what's worth prepping vs what just gets sad in the fridge.

The math of weekday breakfast

Five workdays. Each weekday morning has roughly seven minutes between alarm and door. That's not enough time to actually cook breakfast. So you either skip it (bad), eat something disposable like a granola bar (fine but boring), or you do the cooking on Sunday for thirty minutes and have actual food the whole week. The thirty-minute Sunday version is the one I'm going to convince you to try.

Below is the routine I run most weeks. It scales for one person, two people, or a family.

The 30-minute session, timed

Here is the actual sequence I run. The trick is overlapping the active and passive time so the oven and stovetop are both working while I prep the next thing.

  • 0-2 min: Heat oven to 375°F. Set out a 9x9 baking dish, a large saucepan, and a 6-cup container.
  • 2-12 min: Mix the baked oatmeal (the apple cinnamon recipe is my default). Get it into the oven.
  • 12-15 min: Hard-boil 6 eggs. Bring water to a boil while the oatmeal goes in, drop in eggs, set a 9-minute timer.
  • 15-22 min: Make a jar of overnight oats (the second format below).
  • 22-30 min: Pre-portion fruit (cut up melon or strawberries). Wash and dry berries. Pull the eggs out, ice-bath them.
  • 30+ min: Pull the baked oatmeal when it's done. Let everything cool. Put lids on. Refrigerate.

That's breakfast for five days for one person, or three days for two adults. Active time: about 12 minutes. Passive time: 18 minutes (oatmeal baking, eggs cooking, oats soaking).

The three formats I rotate

1. Baked oatmeal (slice it like cake)

Detailed in the apple cinnamon baked oatmeal recipe. Make one tray on Sunday, slice into six squares, reheat each weekday morning with a splash of milk in the microwave. Lasts five days. Variations endless.

2. Overnight oats (no cooking)

1/2 cup rolled oats + 2/3 cup milk + 1 tablespoon chia seeds + 1 tablespoon maple syrup + pinch of salt. Stir in a jar, refrigerate overnight, eat cold or warm. Lasts 4 days in the fridge. Add fruit and toppings each morning.

3. Hard-boiled eggs (savory protein)

Six eggs covered with cold water, brought to a hard boil, simmered 9 minutes, ice-bath. Peel as you eat them, salt and pepper, a slice of toast. Lasts 1 week, peeled or unpeeled.

Together these three give you sweet (oatmeal), cold/quick (overnight oats), and savory/protein (eggs) options. Rotate or mix them across the week.

What's actually worth prepping

  • Baked goods that hold: baked oatmeal, banana bread, muffins, scones (yes scones).
  • Cooked grains: a pot of steel-cut oats, refrigerated, reheated with a splash of milk.
  • Hard-boiled eggs: peeled or unpeeled, lasts a week.
  • Yogurt-based: yogurt parfaits in jars (assemble morning of for crunch).
  • Savory: mini frittatas in muffin tins, freeze well, reheat in microwave.
  • Smoothie kits: bag the ingredients in freezer bags, dump in blender with milk in the morning.

What gets sad in the fridge (don't bother)

  • Pancakes and French toast. They're decent reheated but never as good as fresh. Make small batches the day you want them.
  • Avocado toast. Avocado oxidizes. Mash fresh.
  • Eggs in any non-hard-boiled form. Scrambled, fried, omelet — all suffer in the fridge.
  • Cereal with milk pre-mixed. Rookie mistake. Cereal goes soggy in 4 minutes.
  • Toast. Toast it the morning you eat it. Pre-toasted toast is just sad.

The morning routine

This is the payoff. Here's what a weekday morning looks like once Sunday is done:

  • Pull a square of baked oatmeal. Microwave 30 seconds with a splash of milk. Add fresh fruit on top.
  • OR scoop a jar of overnight oats. Eat cold from the jar, or warm 60 seconds. Top with whatever's around.
  • OR peel two hard-boiled eggs. Toast a slice of bread, mash an egg with salt and pepper, eat in 90 seconds.

Total weekday breakfast time: 2-3 minutes. The savings are not just in time — they're in not having to think.

The 30-minute session

Bake oatmeal (30 min hands-off)
Boil 6 eggs (10 min)
Make overnight oats jars (5 min)
Cut fruit (5 min)

Result: 5 weekday mornings of real breakfast, 2-3 minutes each.
