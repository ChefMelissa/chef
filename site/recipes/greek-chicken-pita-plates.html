Greek Chicken Pita Plates

Yogurt-marinated chicken charred in a skillet, warm pitas, two-ingredient tzatziki, chopped Greek salad. The yogurt marinade does most of the work — even 20 minutes makes a real difference.

Total
30 min + marinade
Yield
4
Pan
cast-iron

Why I make this

This is the dinner I make when summer hits and the oven is the enemy. The chicken cooks in a single skillet in about ten minutes, the salad is chop-and-dress, the tzatziki is two ingredients, and the pitas warm in the same pan that just held the chicken. Plates go to the table, everyone builds their own. The kitchen doesn't get hot.

The yogurt marinade is the only step that requires planning. It sounds gimmicky but it's not — the lactic acid in yogurt slowly tenderizes chicken in a way that vinegar or citrus marinades can't. Twenty minutes is the minimum; an hour is better; overnight is incredible. I make the marinade in the morning and put it in the fridge with the chicken. By dinner, all I do is cook.

The recipe

Prep 15 minCook 15 minMarinate 20 min minimumYield 4 servingsPan cast-iron skillet

For the chicken

  • 1.5 lb (700 g) boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1.5-inch pieces
  • 3/4 cup full-fat Greek yogurt
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • 3 garlic cloves, grated on a microplane
  • 1.5 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper

For the tzatziki

  • 1 cup Greek yogurt
  • 1/2 cucumber, grated and SQUEEZED in a clean towel
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1/2 tsp salt

For the salad and serving

  • 1 cucumber, diced 1/2-inch
  • 2 medium tomatoes, diced
  • 1/2 red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup crumbled feta
  • 1/4 cup pitted Kalamata olives
  • 2 tbsp olive oil + pinch oregano + pinch salt
  • 4 pita breads (the puffy kind, not the pocket-flat kind ideally)

Method

  1. Marinate the chicken. Whisk yogurt, olive oil, lemon juice, garlic, oregano, salt, pepper in a bowl. Add chicken pieces, stir to coat. Cover and refrigerate at least 20 minutes; up to 4 hours. (Past 4 hours the texture starts to feel "spongy.")
  2. Make tzatziki. Grate the cucumber on a box grater. Wrap in a clean kitchen towel and SQUEEZE hard over the sink — you want a tablespoon or two of liquid out. Mix with yogurt, garlic, lemon juice, salt. Refrigerate while you finish.
  3. Chop the salad. Combine cucumber, tomato, red onion, feta, olives. Dress with olive oil, oregano, pinch of salt. Don't add the dressing more than 10 minutes before eating or the tomatoes weep.
  4. Cook the chicken. Heat a cast-iron skillet over medium-high until very hot (smoke point of a drop of water is gone in 2 seconds). DON'T add extra oil — the marinade has plenty. Cook chicken in two batches if needed (don't crowd) for 3 minutes per side. The yogurt browns into a beautiful char.
  5. Warm the pitas. Wipe out the skillet, lower heat to medium. Warm each pita 30 seconds per side. Stack on a plate, cover with a clean towel.
  6. Plate and assemble. Pita, chicken, generous spoon of tzatziki, scoop of salad on the side. Eat with your hands.

Melissa's kitchen note

Squeeze the cucumber for tzatziki like you mean it. Wet cucumber turns tzatziki into "yogurt soup" within an hour. A clean kitchen towel works better than paper towels — you can really wring it out. The water you squeeze out goes down the sink; it's full of cucumber flavor but if it's in your tzatziki, it's wrecking the texture.

If you have time, marinate the chicken overnight. The difference between "20 minutes marinade" and "8 hours marinade" is the difference between "good chicken" and "best chicken I've made in months." Yogurt is genuinely magical here.

Ingredient notes

  • Chicken thighs — not breasts. Thighs forgive the long marinade and stay juicy with the high-heat sear. Breasts dry out.
  • Full-fat Greek yogurt — not low-fat (too thin), not skyr (too tangy), not regular yogurt (too watery). Greek-style with at least 5% fat.
  • Pita — the soft, puffy kind from a Mediterranean grocery is best. Supermarket "pocket pita" works but it's drier. Lavash also works.
  • Feta — block feta in brine, not pre-crumbled. The pre-crumbled stuff has anti-caking and tastes like styrofoam.
  • Kalamata olives — pitted. Whole-pitted olives have better texture than the chopped olive crud in the small jars.

Substitutions and variations

  • Lamb instead. 1.5 lb of lamb shoulder, cubed, same marinade. Cook 4 minutes per side. Better than chicken if you can find it.
  • Vegetarian. Use 1 lb halloumi cheese or extra-firm tofu, cubed. Skip the yogurt marinade for halloumi (just olive oil + lemon + oregano). For tofu, press first then marinate.
  • Make wraps instead of plates. Slice the chicken, pile in a pita with tzatziki and salad, fold like a burrito. Travels well for lunch.
  • Add herbs. 2 tbsp chopped fresh dill in the tzatziki, or fresh mint in the salad.
  • Skip the pita. Serve over rice or with cauliflower rice for a low-carb version.

Storage and reheating

Cooked chicken keeps 3 days in the fridge. The salad is best the day you make it. Tzatziki improves overnight (give it 2 days max). Pitas should be eaten the day you warm them; they go leathery in the fridge.

For meal prep: marinate the chicken in advance, cook it, store in the fridge. Cold chicken is excellent on a salad the next day; reheats well in a hot skillet (don't microwave or it goes rubbery).

FAQ

Can I grill instead of skillet?

Absolutely. Thread the marinated chicken onto skewers, grill 4 minutes per side over high heat. Slightly better than skillet because of the actual smoke.

Can I leave out the yogurt in the marinade?

You can sub buttermilk, but it won't be as thick or as tenderizing. Plain marinades (olive oil + lemon + herbs) work but the chicken needs longer to develop flavor.

How do I know the chicken is done?

Cut a piece in half. The juices should run clear, no pink. The internal temp at the thickest part should be 165°F. Charred outside, just-cooked inside is the goal.

Can I freeze the marinated chicken?

Yes — freeze in the marinade in a labeled bag. Thaw overnight in the fridge; the marinating happens during the thaw. Cook from thawed.
